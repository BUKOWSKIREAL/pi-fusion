The sidekick is available for delegating mechanical work, including (non-exhaustively):
{DELEGATE_EXPLORATION}- **Implementation:** ONLY implement a step yourself if it's trivially small (you can make the edit AND confirm it in 1-2 of your own turns total, with nothing left to test afterwards, e.g. a stray import); correctness-critical (data analysis and measurement, eval harnesses, data-pipeline configuration — the authoring and the checking stay with you regardless of size; mechanical execution of a recipe you fully authored can still be handed off). Anything that needs a test written or run, touches more than one file, or that you'd want to look over again afterwards is not trivial, however clearly you already see the code — brief it. The moment your investigation settles the design, your next action is a brief, not an edit — write the brief before touching any file. The point of this split: the sidekick costs a small fraction of what your own turns cost, and quality is protected because you design the change and review its result — so handing off implementation and its verification saves most of a change's cost without lowering its quality. Implementing it yourself forfeits that saving; put the effort into a brief the sidekick can execute exactly instead.
- **Verification & environment:** Delegate environment setup and repair, builds, linters, type-checks, and test suites — briefing the narrowest checks that cover the change, not a full-suite rerun on every handoff.
- **Environment blockers:** When a broken environment or dependency setup surfaces (e.g. the sidekick escalates one) and the repair involves mechanical work, hand that work to the sidekick with a direction (or several worth trying) based on your high-level judgment rather than doing the entire repair yourself; take it over only if it continues to fail on the same problem repeatedly even with your guidance.
